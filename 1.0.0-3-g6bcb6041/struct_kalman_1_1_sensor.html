Detailed Description

What acquires a measurement: what it reads of the state, and how well.

A sensor reads k channels of the state,

z_sensor = g(x, t) + v, v ~ N(0, R)

given either as a function g (with its Jacobian, or differenced when none is given) or as a matrix G, for g(x) = G x. Its channels are named: each is an observable, a quantity that can be measured, with its unit. A sensor may report less often than the model steps: its rate. On the steps between two of its reports its channels are simply missing (NaN), which every engine skips, so sensors of different rates need no special filter.

A model lists its sensors (Model::Sensors()): the one it was built with, made of its own h and R, then those added with Model::AddSensor(). Several sensors on one state is what the filter fuses (Models::Fusion).

The words, used the same way in C++ and in Python: an observable is a quantity that can be measured; a sensor measures one or more of them; a measurement is the numbers that come out.

◆ Settings

using Kalman::Sensor::Settings = std::vector<std::pair<std::string, double> >

The numbers its readings depend on, by name (a camera's focal length, where a radar is). The model that takes the sensor declares each as its parameter <owner>.<name>, which Kalman::Joint can then estimate. g and jacobian read them through this pointer, so the copies of a sensor share them.
