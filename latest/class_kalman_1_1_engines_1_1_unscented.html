Detailed Description

The sigma-point engine (UKF) — nonlinear moments without a Jacobian.

Where Engines::Extended linearises f and h at the mean, this engine pushes a deterministic set of 2n + 1 sigma points through the true maps and recovers mean and covariance from their weighted spread (the unscented transform of Julier & Uhlmann). Nothing is differentiated, so a model with a poor or expensive Jacobian — or none — is handled at second-order accuracy.

The noise is additive: P = sum Wc (Y - x)(Y - x)^T + L Q L^T on prediction and S = sum Wc (Z - z)(Z - z)^T + R on update, which is the form Kalman::Model's f(x, u, t) / h(x, t) contract supports. The Python engine additionally augments the state for non-additive noise; a C++ model that needs that folds the noise into its own f.

alpha, beta, kappa are the usual spread parameters: alpha scales the points around the mean (small, e.g. 1e-3, for a tight cloud), beta folds in prior knowledge of the distribution (2 is optimal for a Gaussian), kappa is a secondary scale (0 by default).

The transition reported to the smoother is the identity, as in the Python engine: a sigma-point pass has no single linearisation to hand back, and the RTS step still runs on it.

Mirrors modules/framework/engines/unscented.py.
