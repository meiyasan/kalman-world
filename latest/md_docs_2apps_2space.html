Space navigation

A spacecraft coasting between worlds, tracked from the ground, and the tools that plan such a trip.

The models

Class Trip Forces
TransLunarModel Earth to the Moon Earth, Moon and Sun
InterplanetaryModel one planet to another (Mars is the worked case) the Sun, plus the pull of the planets
state x = [position, velocity] (megametres and km/s, six numbers)
dynamics velocity' = the sum of the pulls of each body
measurement z = position (ground-station tracking)

Planning

kalman.apps also has the planning tools, which need no filter:

Function What it gives
hohmann_transfer the cheapest two-burn transfer between two circular orbits
lambert the orbit that joins two points in a given time
porkchop the cost of every pair of departure and arrival dates
chain_lambert a trip through several flybys, with what each flyby gives for free and what must be burnt

A flyby changes the direction of the spacecraft at no cost in fuel. chain_lambert reports that free change apart from the burn the trip still needs. With a deep-space manoeuvre placed between the two Venus flybys, the Cassini trip comes out close to the real mission: the notebook compares the two, quantity by quantity.

How it is built

  • Time stepping. One RK4 step per sample, or n_sub of them when the spacecraft passes close to a body and the pull changes quickly.
  • Where the planets are. The Moon and Sun follow circles by default. With use_ephemeris=True, or an epoch for the interplanetary model, their positions come from the real ephemeris.
  • Course corrections. With control=True the interplanetary model takes a small velocity change as its input. The notebook computes these corrections from the filter's estimate and shows what each one removes.
  • C++. TransLunarModel and InterplanetaryModel have twins in libKalmanApps (Kalman::Apps::Astro::TransLunar, ::Interplanetary), so model.filter(z) runs wholly in C++, about 10 times faster. The planets' positions the interplanetary model tabulates from the ephemeris are handed to the twin when it runs. A course correction (control=True) and an integrator of your own keep the Python step.

Using it

from kalman.apps import hohmann_transfer, chain_lambert
print(hohmann_transfer("earth", "mars")) # cost, flight time, time between launch windows
