Gravitational-wave polarization

A gravitational wave stretches space in two directions at once, and two detector channels record it. The way the two channels combine, the polarization, tells how the source is oriented. The filter follows the amplitude, the phase and the polarization as the wave sweeps up in frequency.

The model

state x = [log amplitude, the phase, the two polarization angles]
(8 numbers: each one with its rate of change)
measurement z = the two channels, real and imaginary parts (4 numbers)

The amplitude is kept as its logarithm so that it cannot turn negative. A negative amplitude with the phase shifted by half a turn is the same signal, and a filter free to choose between the two loses the phase.

How it is built

  • Time stepping. The state moves linearly: one fixed matrix per step.
  • A nonlinear measurement. The channels depend on the state through sines and cosines, so this is an extended Kalman filter. The model gives the derivative of the measurement in closed form.
  • It runs in C++. filter() hands the whole pass to the C++ twin Kalman::Apps::Gravitational::Polarization (registered polarization; see kalman model polarization for its components and units). The noise matrices and the sample rate are pushed from the Python model; turning a complex signal into four real columns (preprocess) stays in Python.
  • The signals are in toy units: an amplitude near 1 and a few hertz. A real wave has a strain near 1e-21 between ten hertz and a kilohertz.

Using it

from kalman.apps import PolarizationModel
wave = PolarizationModel("pair", fs=100.0, sigma_n=1.0)
