Inverse of a symmetric positive-definite matrix.

Cholesky first; on failure the diagonal is jittered by a growing Tikhonov term and retried; if that still fails, a pseudo-inverse is built from the symmetric eigendecomposition, dropping directions whose eigenvalue is below a relative tolerance. That last step is the dependency-free equivalent of numpy's pinv, and it is what keeps a degenerate innovation covariance from turning into NaN.

Parameters
Sthe matrix to invert; only its symmetric part is used.
okwhen given, reports whether the plain (unjittered) Cholesky worked.

The solution of the Stein (discrete Lyapunov) equation X = A^T X A + W.

X = sum_k (A^T)^k W A^k, summed by doubling (X += A_j^T X A_j, A_{j+1} = A_j^2), so 2^j terms cost j steps.

Exceptions

Decode a continuous linear system into one sample (Van Loan, 1978).

For dx/dt = A x + w, with w white noise of intensity G (per unit time; G = L Qc L^T), gives the transition F = exp(A dt) and the covariance Q of the noise accumulated over dt, exactly, including the cross terms a plain G dt misses. Works for a singular A.
