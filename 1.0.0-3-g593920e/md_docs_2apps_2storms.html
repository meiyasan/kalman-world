Storm tracking

Two models for severe weather: the track and intensity of a tropical cyclone, and the chance that the air is in a state that produces tornadoes.

A storm track with satellite fixes and the estimate, beside the probability of each motion regime

The storm model

A typhoon, a hurricane and a cyclone are the same phenomenon under regional names, so there is one StormModel. The hemisphere is the only regional setting.

state x = [longitude, latitude, v_east, v_north, intensity]
dynamics the velocity turns at the rate ω (ω = 0: straight)
the centre moves with the velocity plus the beta drift
the intensity persists
measurement z = [longitude, latitude, intensity] (a satellite fix)

The beta drift is the slow push toward the pole and the west that the Earth's rotation gives every such storm. A storm that runs straight and one that recurves differ only in the turn rate, so they are two regimes of one model.

The tornado model

A tornado is too small and short-lived to track. What can be estimated is whether the environment favours one. TornadoEnvironmentModel has a single state that relaxes toward the level typical of its regime:

x' = μ + a (x - μ) μ: the regime's level, a: how long the air remembers

How they are built

  • Time stepping. Neither model uses an integrator. A turn at a constant rate has an exact solution over one step, and the model writes it out.
  • Regimes. The filter does not know which regime the storm is in. StormModel.ikf_bank() returns one model per regime (straight, recurving, stalled) and the chance of switching between them. The Interactive Kalman Filter (IKF, the Interacting Multiple Model filter of the literature) runs them together and weighs each by how well it explains the fixes. For the tornado model the switching chances come from the season.
  • Forecast. forecast_cone runs the model forward with no measurements. The growing covariance is the cone of uncertainty drawn on forecast maps.
  • Steering and intensity. A steering wind and an intensity trend can be passed as Python functions. The storm then follows the wind and no longer needs a prescribed turn rate.
  • C++. Both models have twins in libKalmanApps. A steering function keeps the Python step, and so does an intensity function, except the one a LearnedIntensityModel fitted on analytic_environment gives: a ridge regression, whose coefficients the twin takes as arrays.

Using it

from kalman.apps import StormModel, synthetic_track
from kalman.framework.filters import InteractiveKalmanFilter
truth, fixes = synthetic_track(n_steps=40, recurve_at=18, fix_noise=0.3, seed=3)
models, transition, probabilities = StormModel.ikf_bank(fix_noise=0.3)
ikf = InteractiveKalmanFilter(models, transition, initial_probs=probabilities)
estimate = ikf.forward(fixes)
regimes = ikf.mode_probabilities # one column per regime
