Rockets

Four models of one vehicle, from the pad to orbit and back.

Class Phase State
RocketLiftoffModel vertical climb while the propellant burns altitude, speed, mass
RocketAscentModel guided climb to orbit, in two dimensions position and velocity; thrust is the control
RocketDescentModel powered descent of a booster that can tilt position, velocity, tilt, tilt rate, mass
RocketLandingModel the final vertical landing burn altitude, speed, mass

The model

Mass is a state because the rocket burns it:

altitude' = speed
speed' = thrust / mass - gravity - drag / mass
mass' = -burn rate (zero once the propellant is gone)

Even at constant thrust the acceleration grows as the rocket gets lighter. The sensor measures position only. The filter recovers the speed and the remaining mass.

How it is built

  • Time stepping. Each model writes its rate of change and takes one RK4 step per sample, the framework's fourth-order Runge-Kutta integrator.
  • Guidance inside the model. The descent and landing models contain their own autopilot: it throttles the engine to follow a braking profile and tilts the booster toward the pad. The filter therefore predicts a controlled vehicle.
  • Units. Liftoff, descent and landing use metres, seconds and kilograms. The ascent to orbit uses megametres and kiloseconds, so that positions and velocities are numbers of similar size.
  • Process noise. Each model passes L = I, so the noise acts on every state. Without it the framework finds that the step does not respond to noise and applies none.
  • C++. All four have a twin in libKalmanApps (rocket-liftoff, rocket-ascent, rocket-descent and rocket-descent-control, rocket-landing): the derivative, the guidance and the closing step (mass kept at the dry mass, a landed stage held on the pad) in C++, so model.filter(z) and, with a thrust or throttle/gimbal program, model.filter(z, uk) run wholly in Kalman::Filter. A custom integrator, or a subclass that changes the dynamics, keeps the Python physics.

Using it

from kalman.apps import RocketLiftoffModel
rocket = RocketLiftoffModel()
flight = rocket.sample(160.0) # altimeter readings through the burn
estimate = rocket.filter(flight.zk)
print(rocket.metrics(estimate)) # burnout, apogee, peak acceleration
