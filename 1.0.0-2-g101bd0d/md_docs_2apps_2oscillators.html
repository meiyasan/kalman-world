Oscillators

A signal that contains narrow lines: a resonance ringing in a detector, a tone in a recording. The filter follows the amplitude and phase of each line, so the line can be measured or removed.

The model

A line at frequency f0 is slow to change in amplitude and phase. Multiplying the signal by a wave at a nearby carrier frequency fc moves the line down to near zero frequency, where it becomes a slowly turning arrow: its envelope.

state x = [Re, Im] of the envelope
dynamics the arrow turns at f0 - fc and shrinks at the damping rate
measurement z = the demodulated signal

The damping rate comes from the quality factor Q_f: the larger it is, the longer the line rings and the narrower it is.

Class What it tracks
AnalyticOscillatorModel one line, from a complex signal
OscillatorModel the lines inside one frequency window of a real signal
OscillatorBankModel several windows of the same signal at once

How it is built

  • Time stepping. The motion of the envelope is linear, so its step is exact: the matrix exponential of the turn and the decay over one sample.
  • A window is a mask. OscillatorModel takes a list of candidate frequencies and keeps only those inside fc ± dfc. The signal is demodulated and its sample rate reduced before the filter sees it, and the measurement noise is rescaled to match.
  • Lines that share a window are estimated together by one filter, which is what separates two lines closer than the window is wide.
  • A bank sends the same signal to every window.
  • It runs in C++. A window and a bank are composites of constant matrices: block-diagonal F and Q, and an H that sums the lines of a window ([I I ...]) or stacks the windows. The composite says so (its structure is MATRICES), so filter() runs the linear Kalman pass and the smoother on those matrices in C++ (kf_forward_linear, kf_backward_rts), and sample() runs its state chain there too. The demodulation, the decimation and the reconstruction (preprocess, reconstruct) are signal processing around the filter and stay in numpy and scipy.

Using it

from kalman.apps import OscillatorModel
window = OscillatorModel("mic", frequencies=[180.0], fc=180.0, dfc=5.0, Q=1e4, fs=4096)
estimate = window.filter(signal) # signal: the real samples

Reconstruction: resampling artifacts and their fixes

The rotating-frame oscillator models (OscillatorModel and friends, notebook 02_Oscillators) run the filter at a decimated rate fs_dec = 4·dfc and reconstruct the band signal by upsampling the phasor estimate back to the original rate and remodulating: heterodyne → downsample → Kalman → upsample → remodulate. The up/down resampling (scipy.signal.resample_poly) introduced two artifacts that have since been fixed in OscillatorModel._resample / reconstruct.

1. Asymmetric tail decay in the reconstructed time series

Symptom. The reconstructed waveform matched the truth at the start but the end collapsed to ~zero (the envelope decayed away over the last fraction of a second), while the head was fine — a left/right asymmetry.

Cause. resample_poly implicitly zero-pads beyond the data, so its FIR runs into zeros at the boundaries. On upsampling this is asymmetric: the leading edge recovers (the FIR step-onset settles to the right value) but the trailing ~half-filter of output ramps linearly into the zero-pad, suppressing the tail. The Kalman filter and smoother were not the cause — a bare resample_poly round-trip reproduced it.

Fix. _resample now edge-replicates before resampling: it pads both ends with the edge sample, resamples the padded signal, then trims the padded region back out, so the FIR always sees real data across the original span. Output length is unchanged. Result: tail tracks truth (relative RMS error ~0.4% vs a full tail collapse).

‍Subtlety — the pad must be a whole number of down-blocks. Trimming the pad back out by a rounded offset round(pad·up/down) leaves a fractional-sample group delay (~2 ms in the violin-mode setup). A delay δt rotates every off-carrier phasor by 2π·Δf·δt, so an on-resonance (DC) envelope is unaffected but a detuned mode picks up a phase error that grows with Δf — silently capping the subtraction of detuned modes at ~30 dB. Padding by a multiple of down makes pad·up/down an exact integer (zero delay); detuned single-mode subtraction then reaches 50–60 dB, and the 3-peak FUSED demo goes from ~30 dB to ~40–54 dB.

2. "Bad suppression range" — apparent leakage outside the window

Symptom. In the subtraction ASD the reconstruction (the line being subtracted) appeared to spread a broad skirt far beyond the analysis window [fc ± dfc], and two small spurious spikes sat at fc ± fs_dec, suggesting the filter was acting (and subtracting) well outside its declared band.

Cause — two separate effects:

  • Most of the "skirt" was FFT leakage, not real content. An unwindowed periodogram of a strong narrow line has 1/Δf sinc sidelobes spanning the whole axis; they are identical for the truth and the reconstruction (same tone), so the reconstruction was faithful, not leaking. Applying a window (e.g. Hann) before the FFT collapses the skirt and reveals the genuine band-edge drop at [fc ± 2·dfc] (the decimated Nyquist). The window [fc ± dfc] is a mode-selection mask, not a brick-wall on the output.
  • The two spikes at fc ± fs_dec were real upsampling images. Upsampling from fs_dec leaves spectral copies of the band at ±k·fs_dec, which the anti-image filter only partially suppresses. resample_poly's default Kaiser window (β = 5) left the first image at ≈ −73 dB.

Fix. Because the carried signal lives in [fc ± dfc] while the decimated Nyquist is 2·dfc (a full octave of guard band), a steeper stopband is essentially free: _resample now uses a sharp Kaiser β = 12 anti-alias/anti-image filter, dropping the first image to ≈ −125 dB with no change to in-band content. For pristine output, reconstruct(record, band_limit=True) additionally low-passes the upsampled phasor at 2·dfc, erasing the fc ± fs_dec images entirely (≈ −145 dB, below the FFT floor); it is opt-in and off by default. When plotting these spectra, window the FFT so the true band limit is visible rather than rectangular-window leakage.
